# Conversation Guide: Kanitha Chim - July 13, 2026

## The Situation

Kanitha told you this morning she's exploring internal mobility. Her words:
"nothing concrete yet", "just looking", "no specific date", "Toolchain is my
priority." This is an early signal - she's telling you before acting, which
shows trust. You have a window, but it requires action, not just reassurance.

This is a continuation of unresolved themes from March. She's not suddenly
unhappy - she's been sitting with these questions for 4 months.

Your development brief shows she's objectively at the IC3-IC4 boundary with
strong momentum. The QMS audit SME work, the TPO role, the cross-project
coordination (4 Jira projects, 25 Slack channels, broadest reach on ATC) -

## Conversation Flow

### 1. Open with honesty (2-3 min)

Thank her for telling you early. Acknowledge you went back to the March
career development notes and recognized that several things you committed
to weren't closed.

> "Kanitha, thank you for being upfront with me. I went back to our career
> development discussion from March and I owe you an honest acknowledgment -
> there were several things I said I'd follow up on that I didn't close the
> loop on. That's on me."

Name the specific items:
- PO/TPO training through the PO Chapter
- Leadership learning options
- Offloading distribution so you can explore more
- Protected time for Gator stabilization

Don't over-apologize. State it, own it, move forward.

### 2. Understand what's pulling her (10-15 min)

The critical question: is she moving TOWARD something, or AWAY from the
current role? The answer completely changes your response.

Questions to ask (pick 2-3, not all):

- "When you say you're looking for something interesting - do you have a
  sense of what 'interesting' looks like? Is it a different technology, a
  different kind of role, a different team dynamic?"

- "In March you said you wanted to 'try out more things and figure out what
  I really want to do.' Has that gotten clearer in the last few months, or
  is it still open?"

- "Is there something about the work itself that's not working, or is it
  more about how the role feels day to day?"

- "You mentioned the TPO role makes it hard to see your impact. If we
  changed how the TPO role works for you - or if you stepped back from it -
  would that change how you feel about being on the team?"

**Listen for:** Does she name a specific type of work she wants? Does she
mention people, culture, growth ceiling? Does she reference the TPO role
specifically? These tell you whether the fix is structural (change her role)
or fundamental (she needs a different environment).

### 3. Show her what you see (5 min)

She said she wants to be remembered as "a decent and capable colleague."
That's underselling herself dramatically. Show her the evidence without
making it feel like a pitch to stay.

Key data points to share:

- "You're the team's distribution anchor across 4 Jira projects. That's
  the broadest reach of anyone on ATC."

- "The QMS audit work you took on voluntarily - coordinating with 6+
  stakeholders, preparing and presenting to TUV SUD - that's not 'decent
  colleague' work. That's someone operating at the next level."

- "Hubert and Eitan independently called out your TPO role as valuable.
  Sabine said she reads 'great respect and value for her and her work.'
  This isn't me being nice - this is the organization telling you
  something."

- "From a career framework perspective, you're showing Level 4 signals in
  scope, complexity, and impact. The main gap is design leadership
  artifacts - not ability."

**Don't say:** "You're too valuable to lose" (pressure). **Do say:** "I
want to make sure you're making this decision with a full picture of where
you stand" (information).

### 4. Address the open items concretely (5 min)

Don't promise again without dates. For each item, either commit to a date
or be honest that it's not happening:

| Open Item | What to Say |
|---|---|
| PO/TPO training | "I'll contact Julia Williams at the PO Chapter this week. I'll have options for you by [specific date]." |
| Leadership learning | "Let's pick one thing - a course, a mentoring relationship, or a stretch assignment. What appeals to you most?" |
| Distribution offloading | "With Aman onboarding Jul 18 and Konflux maturing, there's a real path to reduce your distribution load. Can we map out what to hand off?" |
| Gator stabilization time | "Let's block time in the next sprint specifically for Gator. I'll make sure it's protected." |
| Technical + leadership skill specifics | "In March I asked what specific technical and leadership skills you felt were missing and we never came back to it. Can we go there now?" |

### 5. Explore what staying could look like (5 min)

Only go here if the conversation energy is right. Don't force it.

- "If you could design your ideal role on this team - or a modified version
  of your current role - what would it look like?"

- "What would need to be true for this to be a 10 out of 10?"

- "Would it help to have a 3-month experiment where we change something
  specific about your role - like stepping back from TPO, or taking on a
  new technical area - before you decide?"

### 6. Close with clear next steps (2 min)

Whatever the outcome, end with specifics:

- "I support your exploration. I mean that - I want you to find the right
  fit, whether that's here or somewhere else at Red Hat."
- Agree on a check-in cadence (biweekly? monthly?) specifically for this
  topic.
- Name the 2-3 things you'll do this week with dates.
- Ask: "Is there anything I haven't asked that you want me to know?"

## Things to Avoid

- **Don't guilt-trip** ("the team needs you", "who will do distribution")
- **Don't minimize** ("I'm sure it'll pass", "everyone feels this way")
- **Don't counter-offer prematurely** (she hasn't received an offer)
- **Don't promise what you can't deliver** - if PO training doesn't exist,
  say so and propose an alternative
- **Don't make it about retention metrics** - make it about her

## What Success Looks Like After This Meeting

- She feels heard AND actioned (not just heard)
- You have clarity on whether this is fixable or fundamental
- There are 2-3 specific commitments with dates, written down
- She knows exactly where she stands on the career framework
- The door stays open for honest ongoing conversation
