## 2. Key Findings from the Conversation

### What Kanitha wants

Kanitha described her ideal work in concrete terms:

- **Architectural design** - how systems should look, scalability, efficiency,
  structuring programs and services
- **Hands-on programming and coding** - building features, making tools that
  are used by others
- **Debugging and pair programming** - collaborating with the team to solve
  problems together
- **Technical decision-making** - choosing libraries, technologies, and
  architectures for specific projects
- **System planning** - how projects should be structured, how gating should
  look, how services should be scalable and efficient

> **Assessment: Structural, not fundamental.** Kanitha has no issues with the
> team, people, or culture. She is not running away from the current
> environment - she is drawn toward a type of work (architecture + coding)
> that her current workload (distribution + TPO) does not leave room for. The
> fix is changing her role composition, not her environment.

### TPO role - shift since March

In March, Kanitha was uncomfortable with the TPO role and could not see its
impact (7/10 satisfaction). In July, her position has shifted:

- She has **grown comfortable** with it and feels she contributes effectively
- She is **willing to reduce** TPO responsibilities but does not want to
  abruptly discard them
- Her preferred approach: offload distribution first, then assess whether TPO
  is also too much
- The TPO is no longer the primary pain point - the real issue is that TPO +
  distribution together consume all her time

### Internal mobility context

- Nothing concrete - she is exploring, not planning to leave
- Driven by a personal need to explore and discover what she wants ("I only
  know whether I like it or not once I do it")
- Not triggered by any recent team events or interpersonal issues
- Has been thinking about this since March but struggled to define what she
  wants

### Team dynamics observation

Kanitha noted that the team works in silos due to individual focus areas,
limiting collective problem-solving on foundational work (gating, pipelines,
distribution). She wishes for more cross-focus-area discussion and
collaboration. This observation directly supports the need for a cross-cutting
architectural role.
