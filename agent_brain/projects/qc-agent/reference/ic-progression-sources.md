# IC Progression Matrix Sources

Reference data sources for development feedback generation. These progression matrices define the expectations for each IC level and are used to assess growth areas and development paths.

## Software Engineer (IC) Progression

**Source:** [Google Sheets - April 2026](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1OAjiTMCBJ4YkBqfh69JwEgW81BJLbAeU8GWanYYCE_4/edit?gid=798132925)

Covers: Associate Trainee SE (Level 1) through Senior Distinguished Engineer (Level 6).

**Applies to:** Most team members with the "Software Engineer" job family.

### Level mapping

| Level | Job Title | Key scope shift |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Associate Trainee Software Engineer | Learning, guided contributions to a subsystem |
| 2 | Associate Software Engineer | Guided contributions to a subsystem |
| 3 | Senior Software Engineer | Independent contributions to a subsystem |
| 4 | Principal Software Engineer | Cross-subsystem/component features, mentors senior engineers, community leader |
| 5 | Senior Principal Software Engineer | Cross-team technical strategy, organizational influence, conference presentations |
| 6 | Distinguished Engineer | Product-level technical vision, industry thought leader, business unit strategy |

### Key differentiators Level 3 to Level 4

- Technical Impact: subsystem -> cross-subsystem/component
- Quality: own code quality -> establish/monitor testing practices for multiple teams
- Community: active contributor -> key representative and leader
- Mentoring: guide new team members -> coach/mentor senior engineers across teams
- Business: proactive feature development -> visible business impact initiatives
- AI: independently apply tools -> evaluate and introduce new methodologies
- SDLC: champion within team -> lead definition for multi-component systems
- Knowledge sharing: team documentation -> blog posts, design docs, conference presentations

### Key differentiators Level 4 to Level 5

- Technical Impact: cross-subsystem -> drives technical strategy across multiple subsystems, influences overall architecture
- Quality: multi-team testing -> large-scale systems, architectural reliability patterns
- Community: team-level leader -> drives innovation with community-first mindset, participates across multiple communities, industry working groups
- Mentoring: coach senior engineers -> across organizations, mentors principal engineers, role models mentorship
- Business: visible business impact -> drives initiatives across organization recognizing end-to-end value delivery
- AI: evaluate methodologies -> drives strategy for integrating advanced AI ecosystems at scale
- SDLC: lead for complex systems -> drives evolution of SDLC within organization
- Knowledge sharing: blog posts, conferences -> frequently presents at conferences, often to larger audiences

## Job Leveling Framework (Radford-based)

**Source:** [Job Leveling Framework PDF - February 2025](file:///home/aefrat/Downloads/Job%20Leveling%20Framework%20PDF_version%20Feb%202025.pdf)
**Local copy:** `~/Downloads/Job Leveling Framework PDF_version Feb 2025.pdf`

Red Hat uses the Radford leveling methodology with three career tracks: Management, Professional, and Support. All IC engineers (Software, Quality, SRE) are on the **Professional track**. Job leveling defines what's expected at each level across three dimensions: Scope, Complexity, and Impact.

### Professional Track - 3 Dimensions by Level

#### Scope (functional reach, breadth, guidance provided)

| Level | Scope |
|-------|-------|
| 3 (Senior) | Works independently with minimal instruction. Delivers on/enhances existing processes or products to support business priorities. Coordinates activities of others (team lead). Mentors and provides guidance to new team members |
| 4 (Principal) | Works independently to set and achieve objectives tied to functional targets and goals. Proposes new techniques and methods based on business context that have impact within the function. Coordinates activities of other team members (team lead) to ensure successful completion. Mentors and provides guidance to new team members |
| 5 (Sr Principal) | Works independently to set and achieve mid-term objectives aligned to Red Hat company business goals. Drives vision and innovation, determining best procedures for assignments that have cross-functional impact. Leads activities of other professional staff (team lead), serving as expert regional or functional resource. Mentors and provides guidance to colleagues in own discipline |

#### Complexity (judgment, expertise, relationships)

| Level | Complexity |
|-------|------------|
| 3 (Senior) | Good judgment independently on non-routine, moderately complex issues in own area. Recognized as experienced professional, advises less experienced peers. Productive working relationships within discipline; some interaction with senior colleagues. Min 5 years experience |
| 4 (Principal) | Independent judgment on varied, non-routine projects; resolves complex issues with creative solutions impacting overall function. Recognized as expert within team/department with extensive mastery of functional, business, or technical knowledge. Productive working relationships within function, leads cooperative efforts among teams. Interacts with senior colleagues and customers frequently. Min 8 years experience |
| 5 (Sr Principal) | Independent judgment and business acumen on highly complex issues impacting critical projects, valuable relationships, and key business processes. Recognized as subject matter expert, applies broad technical knowledge across disciplines to advise functional leaders and customers on strategy. Formal networks across Red Hat to influence priorities and objectives both internally and with customers; consistently reinforces "One Red Hat" mindset. Min 12 years experience |

#### Impact (accountability, strategy, decisions)

| Level | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| 3 (Senior) | Contributes to team and discipline's goals through completion of tasks. Decisions moderately impact work of others on immediate team; may have minimal impact on other internal teams. Works independently on moderate scope projects |
| 4 (Principal) | Contributes to functional goals through operational direction and tactical activities. Decisions impact customer, operational, or program objectives. Begins to serve as project lead or team-level expert for complex issues |
| 5 (Sr Principal) | Activities directly impact function's achievement of medium to long-term results and strategy. Decisions significantly impact direction and resource allocation for customer, operational, or program objectives. Consistently serves as project lead and technical subject matter expert for highly complex issues |

### Key Level Transitions (Professional Track)

**Level 3 to Level 4 shift:**
- From "working independently on tasks" to "setting and achieving objectives tied to functional targets"
- From "enhancing existing processes" to "proposing new techniques and methods"
- From "experienced professional" to "recognized expert within team/department"
- From "productive relationships within discipline" to "leading cooperative efforts among teams"
- From "moderate impact on immediate team" to "decisions impact customer/operational/program objectives"
- From "mentors new team members" to "coordinates activities of team as team lead"

**Level 4 to Level 5 shift:**
- From "functional targets" to "mid-term objectives aligned to Red Hat business goals"
- From "proposes new techniques within function" to "drives vision and innovation with cross-functional impact"
- From "expert within team/department" to "subject matter expert advising functional leaders and customers"
- From "leads cooperative efforts among teams" to "formal networks across Red Hat to influence priorities"
- From "decisions impact operational objectives" to "decisions significantly impact direction and resource allocation"
- From "begins to serve as project lead" to "consistently serves as project lead and SME"

## Job Architecture

**Source:** [Job Architecture - The Source](https://source.redhat.com/career_and_benefits/red_hats_rewards_portfolio/global_compensation/job_architecture) (June 2026)
**Local copy:** `~/Downloads/Job architecture - The Source.html`

Job architecture is the framework for organizing and aligning jobs within Red Hat based on type and level of work. Key concepts:

- **Career Track Pathway:** Shows comparable levels across Management, Professional, and Support tracks. Moving up a Career Milestone Prefix (C1, C2, etc.) constitutes a promotion
- **Promotions include:** Level increases within a track AND moves from IC to people manager (even if the level number decreases)
- **Job families relevant to this team:** Software Engineering (most members), Quality Engineering (Roni), Software Engineering/SRE (Matt)
- **Business titles:** Managers can assign personalized business titles distinct from job profiles. The job profile (Workday title) is what determines level and compensation

### Job Structure for Team's Families

**Software Engineering:**
Associate SE -> SE -> Senior SE -> Principal SE -> Senior Principal SE -> Fellow SE

**Quality Engineering:**
Associate SQE -> SQE -> Senior SQE -> Principal SQE -> Senior Principal SQE -> Distinguished SQE
