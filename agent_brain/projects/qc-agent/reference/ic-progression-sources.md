# IC Progression Matrix Sources

Reference data sources for development feedback generation. These progression matrices define the expectations for each IC level and are used to assess growth areas and development paths.

## Software Engineer (IC) Progression

**Source:** [Google Sheets - April 2026](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1OAjiTMCBJ4YkBqfh69JwEgW81BJLbAeU8GWanYYCE_4/edit?gid=798132925)

Covers: Associate Trainee SE (Level 1) through Senior Distinguished Engineer (Level 6).

**Applies to:** Most team members with the "Software Engineer" job family.

### Level mapping

| Level | Job Title | Key scope shift |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Associate Trainee Software Engineer | Learning, guided contributions to a subsystem |
| 2 | Associate Software Engineer | Guided contributions to a subsystem |
| 3 | Senior Software Engineer | Independent contributions to a subsystem |
| 4 | Principal Software Engineer | Cross-subsystem/component features, mentors senior engineers, community leader |
| 5 | Senior Principal Software Engineer | Cross-team technical strategy, organizational influence, conference presentations |
| 6 | Distinguished Engineer | Product-level technical vision, industry thought leader, business unit strategy |

### Key differentiators Level 3 to Level 4

- Technical Impact: subsystem -> cross-subsystem/component
- Quality: own code quality -> establish/monitor testing practices for multiple teams
- Community: active contributor -> key representative and leader
- Mentoring: guide new team members -> coach/mentor senior engineers across teams
- Business: proactive feature development -> visible business impact initiatives
- AI: independently apply tools -> evaluate and introduce new methodologies
- SDLC: champion within team -> lead definition for multi-component systems
- Knowledge sharing: team documentation -> blog posts, design docs, conference presentations

### Key differentiators Level 4 to Level 5

- Technical Impact: cross-subsystem -> drives technical strategy across multiple subsystems, influences overall architecture
- Quality: multi-team testing -> large-scale systems, architectural reliability patterns
- Community: team-level leader -> drives innovation with community-first mindset, participates across multiple communities, industry working groups
- Mentoring: coach senior engineers -> across organizations, mentors principal engineers, role models mentorship
- Business: visible business impact -> drives initiatives across organization recognizing end-to-end value delivery
- AI: evaluate methodologies -> drives strategy for integrating advanced AI ecosystems at scale
- SDLC: lead for complex systems -> drives evolution of SDLC within organization
- Knowledge sharing: blog posts, conferences -> frequently presents at conferences, often to larger audiences

## SRE (IC) Progression

**Source:** [Google Sheets - April 2026](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1Fcr_ZMjT1hq7CANLFYSRE_9gp0Qcs897rfB-8kpx5PI/edit?gid=1708656902)

Covers: Associate SRE (Level 1) through SRE Distinguished Engineer (Level 6).

**Applies to:** Team members with the "SRE" job family (e.g., Matt Goldman - Principal SRE).

### Key differentiators SRE Level 4 to Level 5

- From managing complex systems -> setting strategic goals aligning with business objectives
- From mentoring junior engineers -> providing technical leadership, mentoring and developing team members
- From incident management -> acting as liaison between SRE and other engineering groups
- From OIDC/security/CI focus -> leading adoption of industry best practices across the organization
- From single-system focus -> leading complex multi-region deployments
- From contributing to SRE tools -> representing SRE in executive-level discussions

## Quality Engineer (IC) Progression

**Source:** [Google Sheets - April 2026](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/11MXcG53p9k6ncsRtxLSF3LuWfne3Z4PL35t1Svps3AM/edit?gid=1630329910)

Covers: Associate QE (Level 1) through Senior Principal QE (Level 5).

**Applies to:** Team members with the "Quality Engineering" job family (e.g., Roni Eliezer - Principal Quality Engineer).

### Key differentiators QE Level 4 to Level 5

- From leading test framework design -> developing and implementing mid-term quality strategies aligned with business goals
- From coordinating team activities -> leading activities of professional staff
- From creative problem-solving -> subject matter expert (regional/functional)
- From mentoring new members -> mentoring peers, providing expert guidance on QE strategy
- From managing CI/CD -> designing enterprise-level CI/CD standards
- From risk identification -> risk mitigation planning and implementation
- From tactical AI use -> strategic AI leverage for workflow optimization
- Key new skill areas: business acumen, stakeholder management, leadership

## Talent Architecture Overview

**Source:** [Global Engineering Talent Architecture - The Source](https://source.redhat.com/departments/products_and_global_engineering/p_and_ge_content/talent_architecture)

The Talent Architecture page is the hub for all job family progression matrices at Red Hat. It links to the Skill Progression Matrices (broken down by Job Family) and the Job Interests Catalog in Workday. The Engineering Competencies (Distinguished Engineer level, IC-6) are a separate framework.

The page also covers the promotion process overview for IC levels 2-5, manager level, and senior director level.
