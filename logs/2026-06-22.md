## Context (session 11, continued — agent-forge prior art research)

- **Agent-forge design principles — prior art analysis.** User asked whether Juanje Ojeda's agent-forge principles are original inventions or derived from existing work. Launched background research agent that traced all 7 principles and key terminology across academic papers, Anthropic docs, AI agent frameworks, and notable blogs.
- **Overall finding: practitioner synthesis with novel terminology.** The foundational insights all have prior art — Anthropic's character alignment research (principles 1, 3), Boris Cherny's instruction budget (principle 2), CoALA memory taxonomy from Princeton/CMU (principle 6), OWASP/Claude Code deny-by-default (principle 7). The value is in packaging: naming unnamed patterns and reframing known empirical findings as deliberate design levers.
- **Genuinely novel coinages (zero public results):** "disconfirmation gate" (packages CoVe + Popperian falsification into a skill-design gate), "computed store" (script-only statistics as a formal memory store type), "skill-permission parity" (isomorphism between skill and permission scope), "channel reliability" (term for instruction hierarchy).
- **Claims that don't hold up:** (1) "uniform degradation" — academic evidence (Liu et al. "Lost in the Middle") shows positionally-biased U-shaped degradation, not uniform. (2) "file nav outperforms RAG up to ~1000 entries" — no published source supports this specific threshold; literature suggests 100-500 for flat indexes. (3) The ~150-200 instruction budget is attributed to Boris Cherny, not Juanje.

## Lessons (session 11)

- Agent-forge is a good example of practitioner synthesis: deeply internalized literature repackaged into operational principles with precise terminology. The pattern — building real systems, extracting heuristics, coining names for unnamed practices — is a legitimate form of contribution even when the underlying insights aren't novel. Worth tracking as a model for how operational knowledge gets created vs. academic knowledge.
